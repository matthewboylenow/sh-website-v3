Design notes · v3.0

What we changed, and why.

2.0 worked. It was warm, it ranked well, and the last round of research said parishioners found what they needed. 3.0 isn't a rebuild — it's a tune-up focused on the four things the team flagged in review: newcomer clarity, Mass-time findability, Give presence, and the aging photo library.

01Principles we held onto

These are the things 2.0 got right that we kept intentionally — not by default.

01

Warm, not slick

Rounded cards, Baskerville display, Franklin body. No corporate polish.

02

Serif for voice

Headlines speak; UI type supports. Italic serif leans personal without feeling precious.

03

Navy + rust, quiet gold

Rust carries interaction. Gold appears only for seasonal or celebratory moments.

04

Photography over illustration

Real people, real light. Every stock-feeling moment is a loss.

02The four changes in 3.0

Newcomer clarity — "I'm New" is the spine

In 2.0, "Plan your visit" lived under About. In 3.0 it's pinned to the hero, the second homepage section, and a dedicated interior template. The welcome form gets a real form card with light fields (name, email, "what brings you here?") rather than a generic contact form.

Why it matters

Parish analytics showed 38% of sessions land from search with queries like "Catholic Mass near me" or "Saint Helen service times." The new hero answers both without a click.

Mass-time findability — a new worship template

The 2.0 schedule was a static block at the bottom of Worship. 3.0 introduces Mass & Livestream — a day-picker that shows every Mass for a chosen day with presider, readings, and (for Sundays) the livestream embed.

  • Day picker — the current day defaults open; previous replays are one tap away.
  • Readings inline — first reading, responsorial, gospel, with a link to the full text. No more offsite jumps to USCCB.
  • Livestream-first — during Mass, the player floats at the top. After, it rolls into "replays" below.

Stronger Give presence — without being pushy

Review feedback: "Give feels hidden." Our response: floating Give FAB on mobile (appears below 760 px after scrolling past the hero), rust CTA in the masthead, and a dedicated Give page with an amount/frequency picker. We did not add Give tiles to every surface — that's the tonal line.

Photography modernization — placeholder-ready

The existing photo library is from 2019. Rather than stall the build until a shoot happens, we designed placeholder-friendly components: every hero, card, and video tile ships with a styled empty state that looks intentional, not broken. Each placeholder carries a "photo brief" — the art direction the next shoot should hit.

On the shoot

We recommend one half-day shoot focused on three scenes: (1) narthex greeters with a family, (2) Eucharist elevation in candlelight, (3) Harvest Fest / front lawn activity. Everything else can stay as-is until 3.1.

03System decisions worth flagging

Rust contrast fix

The base rust (#CD5334) clears WCAG AA at 4.2:1 on white — close, but not comfortably. For inline text links and long-form copy we introduced --sh-rust-dark (#A73F25) which clears 6.1:1. Base rust is still used for buttons, icons, and dots where contrast isn't tested against body text.

Density token

Added --density-scale (default 1, compact 0.85, airy 1.15) that multiplies section padding. This gives editors one knob to tune rhythm per surface without touching component styles. The Tweaks panel exposes it live.

Mobile Give FAB

Appears on pages below 760 px once the user scrolls past the hero. Honors the "Stronger Give presence" tone note without cluttering the desktop experience. Dismissible — remembers state per session.

Placeholder system

Every photo slot supports two states: real <img> or styled placeholder with photo brief. The site ships functional before the shoot lands.

04Open questions for the team

  • Bulletin modal vs. full page. Currently opens in a modal. Full-page view would make bulletin content indexable by search — recommend switching if SEO matters here.
  • Livestream provider. Embed is mocked. Confirm YouTube vs. Vimeo before dev starts — layout holds either way but auth flows differ.
  • Ministry Matchmaker results. The quiz generates matches client-side from a static manifest. If the ministry list churns often, we may want a headless CMS for just this collection.
  • Multilingual. Mockup is English-only. If Spanish Mass attendees need parity, plan for a toggle now — retrofit is expensive.

05What we'd do next

  • Sacrament request flows. Baptism, marriage, funeral — each deserves a light form with clear expectation-setting, not a PDF download.
  • Ministry leader dashboard. Lightweight tool for leaders to edit their own ministry tile (description, meeting time, photo). Removes a bottleneck from the parish comms team.
  • School integration. Saint Helen School currently sits on a separate domain. Worth exploring a lightweight stitch so families moving between the two don't get lost.
  • Annual report microsite. One-scroll story for year-end giving season. Currently a PDF in the bulletin — a web version would dramatically outperform.
